MONKEY TRACKER
Product Requirements Document — Release 1 candidates

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft for review. Nothing here is committed.
Extends: PRD.txt v1.0. Section numbers below are local to this document;
         cross-references to the base PRD are written as "base 11.x".


0. HOW TO READ THIS

Release 0 shipped presence: one couple, expiring updates, optional location,
an illustrated stage. It works, and it is boring. Every feature here exists to
answer the same complaint — that opening the app when nothing has changed
gives you nothing — without reaching for the two things the product has
already refused: pressure to post, and precision about where someone is.

Three features are specified in full (sections 2-4). They are the ones worth
building. The rest (section 5) are specified tightly enough to argue about and
estimate, but not yet worth full treatment.

Every feature in this document must satisfy the base PRD's standing rules:

- Optional and off by default if it touches location in any form.
- No guilt, no punishment, no streaks, no shaming a partner for not sharing.
- Expired means unknown. Never imply a stale state is current.
- No exact location, private captions, or sensitive status content in
  analytics, logs, notification previews, or AI context.
- Not an emergency, safety-monitoring, or proof-of-location service.

A feature that cannot satisfy those is cut, not negotiated. Section 6 records
two ideas that failed this test and one place where the base PRD already
fails it.


1. RELEASE GOAL

Make the app worth opening when your partner has not just posted.

Today the stage is static between updates. Everything below either adds
ambient motion that nobody has to author (section 2), gives the two people a
reason to be in the app at the same time (section 3), or turns the emptiest
moment in the product into the warmest one (section 4).


2. LOCAL SKY

2.1 Summary

Each half of the shared stage runs on its partner's own local clock, and
optionally their own weather. Their side goes amber at their sunset and dark
at their night. Rain streaks their sky when it is raining on them.

2.2 Problem

For a long-distance couple in different timezones, the single most useful
fact — "is it a reasonable hour for them right now?" — is currently absent
from a product whose entire job is answering "what is my person up to?".
Users compute it in their heads or get it wrong and text at 3am.

Second: the stage is visually identical whether a partner posted four minutes
ago or four hours ago. Ambient change makes the app feel alive without asking
anyone to author anything.

2.3 User story

As someone twelve hours out of sync with my partner, I open the app, see that
their side is dark and quiet, and decide not to call — without reading a
number, doing arithmetic, or being told what to do.

2.4 Behavior

Two independent tiers, separately consented:

Tier 1 — Daylight
- Derived from the partner's device timezone offset only.
- Drives sky color, sun/moon position, and star field across the four bands:
  dawn, day, dusk, night.
- Transitions are continuous, not stepped, so the stage drifts over a day.
- No location permission is required or requested.

Tier 2 — Weather
- Derived from a coarse locality (city-level or coarser, never coordinates).
- Adds one weather layer over the sky: clear, cloud, rain, snow, fog.
- Requires its own explicit opt-in, separate from Tier 1 and separate from
  the existing location precision control.

2.5 Privacy

This is the section that decides whether the feature ships.

- A timezone offset is not a coordinate, but it does narrow someone's
  longitude to a band, and for a couple who already know roughly where each
  other live it reveals travel. It is therefore treated as shared personal
  data: Tier 1 is a consent surface, off by default, revocable in one tap.
- Tier 2 is location data, full stop. It obeys every rule the existing
  location precision control obeys: off by default, plainly labeled, never
  required for an update, and never resolved finer than a locality.
- Neither tier may be inferred from an IP address. Both come from an explicit
  device signal the user has agreed to share.
- Turning either tier off must take effect on the partner's stage promptly,
  and must not notify the partner that it was turned off. Silent revocation
  is the point; a "your partner stopped sharing daylight" notice converts a
  privacy control into a social cost.
- Weather and daylight are excluded from notification previews. "It is
  raining on your partner" is a location claim in a lock-screen banner.

2.6 Expiry semantics

Daylight and weather are ambient, not status. They are properties of the
person, not of an update, and therefore:

- They continue to render after a status expires. An unknown status does not
  black out the sky.
- They must never be mistaken for a status. The unknown state's blank-page
  treatment of the figure is unchanged; only the sky behind it keeps moving.
- If the ambient signal itself goes stale (no device contact for a period to
  be set in 2.9), the sky returns to the neutral daylight-agnostic field used
  today. It does not freeze on the last known sky, which would be the same
  category of lie as a stale status.

2.7 Data model

- Ambient state belongs to the member, not the update: a small record per
  troop member carrying tz offset, optional locality key, optional weather
  code, and a freshness timestamp.
- It is written on app foreground and on a low-frequency background refresh,
  not per update.
- Locality key is stored as an opaque coarse identifier, never a lat/long
  pair, so that a database leak cannot be replayed into coordinates.

2.8 Out of scope

- Forecasts. This is a mood light, not a weather app.
- Any UI that names the partner's city or timezone in text.
- Severe-weather alerting. That is the safety-service line the product does
  not cross.

2.9 Open questions

- Staleness window before the sky reverts to neutral. Long enough that a
  phone in a pocket overnight does not blank the stage; short enough that a
  week-old sky never shows.
- Does daylight follow the viewer's own night too, or only the partner's?
  Symmetry is prettier; asymmetry is more useful.
- Weather provider, and whether it can be queried without sending anything
  more precise than a locality.

2.10 Success signal

Sessions that open, look, and close without posting should feel worth it.
Measured, if at all, by retention of the ambient toggles once enabled — not
by session counts, which would reward exactly the compulsive checking this
product is trying not to build.


3. SIT WITH ME

3.1 Summary

A mutual, time-boxed mode in which both monkeys move onto one shared stage
doing the same activity. The divider dissolves. It ends on its own.

3.2 Problem

Couples apart already do this badly: a video call left running with nobody
talking, purely for the sense of company while working, reading, or cooking.
Nothing in Monkey Tracker currently gives two people a reason to be present at
the same moment, and presence-at-the-same-moment is the only thing the product
cannot fake with an expiring status.

3.3 User story

We are both working, four thousand miles apart. I start a session, they accept,
and for fifty minutes our monkeys are at the same desk. Neither of us says
anything. It ends by itself and neither of us has to end it.

3.4 Behavior

- Either partner proposes a session: an activity, a scene, and a duration.
- The invitation expires on its own if unanswered. It does not persist, does
  not badge, does not repeat, and its expiry is never surfaced to the sender
  as a refusal.
- On acceptance both monkeys render on a single undivided stage in the shared
  scene, each keeping their own fur, face, accessory, and mood.
- The session ends at its duration. Either person may leave early with one
  tap; leaving is silent to the other beyond the stage returning to two halves.
- While a session is live, both statuses are implicitly current: the session
  itself is the update. When it ends, both return to normal expiry rules.
- A gentle shared marker of elapsed time is permitted. A countdown, a
  progress bar, or anything that reads as a target is not.

3.5 Privacy and consent

- Strictly mutual. No unilateral entry, ever.
- Declining is one tap, is never counted, is never shown to the other person
  as a decline, and is indistinguishable from not having seen the invite.
- No session history is retained beyond the standard 30-day timeline, and a
  session entry is deletable by either participant like any other update.
- No content, caption, or activity from a session enters notification
  previews beyond the invitation itself, which names only the activity.

3.6 Failure modes

- Connection loss mid-session must degrade to "the session is still notionally
  running" rather than yanking the partner off the stage, then resolve at the
  scheduled end. A partner vanishing because of a dropped socket reads as
  rejection, and the product must not manufacture that feeling.
- Simultaneous proposals from both sides collapse into one session rather
  than two competing invitations.
- A proposal to a partner whose status is unknown is allowed. Absence is not
  a reason to block an invitation, and the invitation is not a nudge to post.

3.7 Out of scope for the first version

- Audio, video, or text chat inside the session. Other apps do that; the
  entire point here is company without conversation.
- Sessions with anyone other than the one paired partner.
- Scheduling sessions in advance.

3.8 Open questions

- Default durations. A 50/10 pomodoro shape is tempting but imports a
  productivity frame the product has deliberately avoided.
- Whether a session can be started from a widget or a watch.
- What the stage does when the two people choose incompatible scenes; see
  the related "same scene dissolves the divider" idea in 5.2, which may be
  the better primitive underneath this feature.


4. SOMETHING WAITING

4.1 Summary

When a status expires and a partner's half of the stage goes to the unknown
state, the other person may place one small drawn object there. It waits for
them and is present when they return.

4.2 Problem

Every presence product treats absence as a void to be filled by nagging the
absent person. Monkey Tracker already refuses to nag, which leaves the unknown
state honest but inert — a blank page and nothing to do with it.

This feature makes the emptiest moment in the product into the one place your
partner can leave you something. It is the strongest expression of the app's
stated position that not sharing is a complete and acceptable answer.

4.3 User story

They have been unknown since this morning. I leave a small plant on their
empty stage. When they next open the app it is there, and the first thing the
app has told them is that someone thought about them — not that they are
overdue to post.

4.4 Behavior

- Available only while the recipient's status is expired or absent.
- One object at a time per person. Placing a new one replaces the old one.
- Objects come from the existing drawn decor set, extended as needed. No text,
  no photos, no captions. The gesture is the message.
- The object persists across the recipient's next update rather than vanishing
  the instant they post; it should feel received, not consumed.
- The recipient may remove it at any time, silently. The sender is never told.
- The object never carries a call to action. No copy anywhere may frame it as
  a reminder, a prompt, or a request to post.

4.5 Privacy and abuse

- Rate limited per sender per day, in the same spirit as the existing poke
  cap, and for the same reason: a gesture that can be repeated indefinitely
  stops being a gift and becomes pressure.
- Notification, if any, is a single quiet message on the recipient's next
  foreground, not a push. The product does not push someone to come look at
  a reminder that they have been absent.
- Nothing about placement enters analytics beyond an anonymous count.

4.6 Expiry semantics

This feature deliberately sits outside the expiry model: the object is not a
status and makes no claim about anyone's current state. That must be visually
unambiguous — it belongs to the scene, never to the figure, and never appears
in the status line, the precision badge, or the timeline as an update.

4.7 Open questions

- Whether the object should itself expire after some days, or stay until
  replaced. Staying is warmer; expiring is more consistent with the rest of
  the product.
- Whether both people can hold an object for each other simultaneously.
- Whether this is reachable from the widget, which is where someone is most
  likely to notice absence in the first place.


5. COMPACT SPECIFICATIONS

Each of these is real but not yet worth a full section. Format is: what it is,
why it earns its place, and the constraint most likely to kill it.

5.1 Widgets and complications
Extends base 11.9.
Home-screen and lock-screen widgets showing the partner's monkey, and a watch
complication showing the face. Highest value per unit of effort in this
document, because the illustration system is already flat, two-tone, and
scales to 44px by design.
Constraint: the widget must render the unknown state as faithfully as the app
does. A widget that shows a stale monkey because it failed to refresh is the
single worst violation of the expiry rule available to us.

5.2 Same scene dissolves the divider
When both partners independently choose the same scene, the stage renders as
one undivided panel. No button, no announcement, no mechanic — you simply
notice. Likely the underlying primitive for section 3.
Constraint: must not become a coordination game. If anyone ever feels they
should pick a scene to match, it has become pressure.

5.3 Trail as a walk, not a map
Replace any map surface for Trail with the monkey walking across the stage,
arriving somewhere at the end. Motion without coordinates.
Constraint: this is the feature most likely to be misread as a safety
service. Copy must be unambiguous that it is a feeling, not a fix.

5.4 Overlap light
A band of light across the stage showing when the two people's waking hours
intersect. Depends on 2.4 Tier 1.
Constraint: must not become a scheduling tool, or it acquires obligations.

5.5 Ambient sound
Five seconds of ambient audio attached to an update, expiring with it.
Constraint: audio is content. It needs the same private-storage handling as
photo postcards, and must never autoplay in a notification.

5.6 Zero-friction input
NFC stickers, shortcut actions, and widget taps that post a preset scene in
one gesture. The best status is one nobody opened an app to set.
Constraint: presets must not drift into automation. An update the user did not
consciously make is a location beacon wearing a costume.

5.7 Postcard as backdrop
An existing private photo postcard becomes the soft, washed-out backdrop of
that person's half of the stage instead of a thumbnail.
Constraint: photos are the most sensitive content in the app. A backdrop is
much more visible than a thumbnail, including to anyone glancing at the phone.

5.8 Doodle replies
A small drawing that appears as a prop on the partner's stage for an hour, in
the illustration system's ink weight.
Constraint: freeform user content needs a moderation story even at a scale of
two people, because two people can still hurt each other.

5.9 Banana toss
A two-tap micro-game across the divider. Absurd, pointless, on-tone.
Constraint: no scores, no records, no streaks. See 6.2.

5.10 The goodnight ritual
Extends base 11.10. Both partners set Asleep; the stage goes to night
together and one light stays on for whoever wakes first.
Constraint: "who went to bed first" must never become legible as a pattern.

5.11 Reaching across
When both partners are live simultaneously, the monkeys lean toward the
divider. Pure ambient delight, no mechanic.
Constraint: none significant. Cheapest charm in this document.

5.12 Ask for Perch
A partner may request a precision upgrade for a bounded window. Approve or
decline in one tap.
Constraint: the app must never surface how often anyone declined, to either
party. Making refusal cheap AND invisible is the whole feature; a decline
count would turn the consent model into a scoreboard.

5.13 Fade styles
Choose how an update expires: straight to unknown, or leaving a residue — the
mug stays on the stage after you have left the café.
Constraint: residue must be visually incapable of being read as current
status. If a residue can be mistaken for presence, it is cut.

5.14 Letters on a state
Write something delivered when the partner is next Free, or next Sleepy.
Constraint: delivery conditions must not create an incentive to set a
particular state. If a letter is waiting behind "Free", the app has just
given someone a reason to lie about being free.

5.15 The monthly spread
Extends the base PRD's P2 "Monkey Recap". An illustrated printable poster
generated from saved moments — also the honest way to keep something past the
30-day retention window.
Constraint: generation must run on data the user already chose to save, not
on a re-scan of expired content.

5.16 The monkey frame
A small e-ink desk object showing the partner's current monkey. The
illustration system would render on e-ink with no redesign.
Constraint: a physical always-on display of someone's presence in a shared
room is a different consent question than a phone screen. It needs its own.


6. REJECTED, AND ONE CONFLICT

6.1 Mood-driven weather — rejected
Combining both partners' moods into the stage's weather is a lovely idea that
quietly makes one person's bad day into the thing that ruined the picture.
That is guilt with extra steps. If mood ever drives the sky, it drives only
your own half, and never darkens it.

6.2 Anything that counts — rejected as a category
Unlocks, wardrobes, collections, and records all drift toward streaks. If the
treehouse is to fill up over time, it fills from variety or from shared
moments, never from consistency, and it never shows a counter.

6.3 Conflict in the base PRD
Base 11.11 "Couple terrarium" specifies that users "earn bananas for posting,
reacting, or completing rituals". Earning a currency for posting is a
participation incentive, which is the mechanism the product's own rules
forbid — the guard that missing a day "does not destroy progress" limits the
damage without removing the incentive. This needs resolving before the
terrarium is built. Recommended resolution: bananas accrue from shared
moments that both people took part in, never from individual posting
frequency.


7. SEQUENCING

Recommended order, and why:

1. Local Sky, Tier 1 only (section 2). Smallest of the three big features,
   needs no new permission, uses the stage that already exists, and is the
   one that changes how the app feels on first open — which is the actual
   complaint.
2. Widgets (5.1). Highest value per unit effort, and it puts the sky where
   people will actually see it.
3. Reaching across (5.11) and same scene dissolves the divider (5.2). Two
   days of work, disproportionate charm, and 5.2 is groundwork for 6.
4. Something waiting (section 4). Independent of the rest, and the strongest
   statement of what this product believes.
5. Local Sky, Tier 2 (weather). Needs the coarse-locality consent surface
   designed properly; do not rush it in behind Tier 1.
6. Sit with me (section 3). The largest build: needs live presence, mutual
   invitation, and session state. Worth it, but not first.


8. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT ANSWER

- Whether any of this changes the Release 0 constraint of exactly one couple.
  Nothing here requires more than two people, and nothing here should be used
  as an argument for relaxing that.
- Cost. Weather lookups, live sessions, and ambient refresh all add backend
  load that the current single-project Supabase setup has never been sized for.
- Native platform work. Widgets, complications, NFC, and e-ink are all outside
  the Expo-managed surface the app currently lives in.

END OF DOCUMENT
