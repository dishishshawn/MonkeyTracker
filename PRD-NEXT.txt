MONKEY TRACKER
Product Requirements Document — Release 1 candidates

Version: 0.2
Status: Draft for review. Nothing here is committed.
Extends: PRD.txt v1.0. Section numbers below are local to this document;
         cross-references to the base PRD are written as "base 11.x".


0. HOW TO READ THIS

Release 0 shipped presence: one couple, expiring updates, optional location,
an illustrated stage. It works, and it is boring. Every feature here answers
the same complaint — that opening the app when nothing has changed gives you
nothing — without reaching for the two things the product has already
refused: pressure to post, and precision about where someone is.

The document is in two parts.

Part A (sections 2-3) is ambient: reasons the app is worth opening when
nobody has done anything. Nothing to author, nothing to answer.

Part B (sections 4-7) is interactive: reasons to open it now. This is the
half that could most easily betray the product, so section 1.2 sets the rule
the whole part is written against.

Section 8 holds compact specifications — real, but not yet worth full
treatment. Section 9 records what was rejected and why.

Every feature must satisfy the base PRD's standing rules:

- Optional and off by default if it touches location in any form.
- No guilt, no punishment, no streaks, no shaming a partner for not sharing.
- Expired means unknown. Never imply a stale state is current.
- No exact location, private captions, or sensitive status content in
  analytics, logs, notification previews, or AI context.
- Not an emergency, safety-monitoring, or proof-of-location service.

A feature that cannot satisfy those is cut, not negotiated.


1. RELEASE GOAL

1.1 The goal

Make the app worth opening when your partner has not just posted.

Part A adds change nobody has to author. Part B gives two people things that
are only possible together, or that are simply fun to do.

1.2 The rule Part B is written against

"Make people want to open the app" is ordinary product language that, applied
literally, produces streaks, nudge notifications, counters, badges, and
variable rewards for posting. Every one of those is forbidden by the rules
above, and the ban is not a technicality — a couples app that manufactures
compulsion is actively harmful to the relationship it claims to serve.

So the pull must come from one of exactly three honest places:

  (a) Someone touched my world. The thing that changed was done by the one
      person I care about, not by a scheduler.
  (b) This is only possible right now. The reward exists in the overlap of
      two people being present, so the app never has to ask for the visit.
  (c) This is fun to do. The interaction is its own reason.

Nothing in Part B may create a fourth reason: obligation.

And the goal is not maximum opens. A couple who open this twice a day and
feel closer is a success. A couple who open it thirty times a day because
they are anxious about missing something is a failure that would look
identical in the metrics. Any measurement plan that cannot tell those two
apart is not a measurement plan.


PART A — AMBIENT


2. LOCAL SKY

2.1 Summary

Each half of the shared stage runs on its partner's own local clock, and
optionally their own weather. Their side goes amber at their sunset and dark
at their night. Rain streaks their sky when it is raining on them.

2.2 Problem

For a long-distance couple in different timezones, the single most useful
fact — "is it a reasonable hour for them right now?" — is currently absent
from a product whose entire job is answering "what is my person up to?".
Users compute it in their heads or get it wrong and text at 3am.

Second: the stage is visually identical whether a partner posted four minutes
ago or four hours ago. Ambient change makes the app feel alive without asking
anyone to author anything.

2.3 User story

As someone twelve hours out of sync with my partner, I open the app, see that
their side is dark and quiet, and decide not to call — without reading a
number, doing arithmetic, or being told what to do.

2.4 Behavior

Two independent tiers, separately consented:

Tier 1 — Daylight
- Derived from the partner's device timezone offset only.
- Drives sky color, sun/moon position, and star field across four bands:
  dawn, day, dusk, night.
- Transitions are continuous, not stepped, so the stage drifts over a day.
- No location permission is required or requested.

Tier 2 — Weather
- Derived from a coarse locality (city-level or coarser, never coordinates).
- Adds one weather layer over the sky: clear, cloud, rain, snow, fog.
- Requires its own explicit opt-in, separate from Tier 1 and separate from
  the existing location precision control.

2.5 Privacy

This is the section that decides whether the feature ships.

- A timezone offset is not a coordinate, but it narrows longitude to a band,
  and for a couple who already know roughly where each other live it reveals
  travel. Tier 1 is therefore a consent surface: off by default, revocable in
  one tap.
- Tier 2 is location data, full stop. It obeys every rule the existing
  location precision control obeys: off by default, plainly labeled, never
  required for an update, never resolved finer than a locality.
- Neither tier may be inferred from an IP address. Both come from an explicit
  device signal the user has agreed to share.
- Turning either tier off takes effect on the partner's stage promptly and
  must not notify the partner that it was turned off. Silent revocation is
  the point; a "your partner stopped sharing daylight" notice converts a
  privacy control into a social cost.
- Weather and daylight are excluded from notification previews. "It is
  raining on your partner" is a location claim in a lock-screen banner.

2.6 Expiry semantics

Daylight and weather are ambient, not status. They are properties of the
person, not of an update, and therefore:

- They continue to render after a status expires. An unknown status does not
  black out the sky.
- They must never be mistaken for a status. The unknown state's blank-page
  treatment of the figure is unchanged; only the sky behind it keeps moving.
- If the ambient signal goes stale (window per 2.9), the sky returns to the
  neutral daylight-agnostic field used today. It does not freeze on the last
  known sky, which would be the same category of lie as a stale status.

2.7 Data model

- Ambient state belongs to the member, not the update: a small record per
  troop member carrying tz offset, optional locality key, optional weather
  code, and a freshness timestamp.
- Written on app foreground and on a low-frequency background refresh, not
  per update.
- Locality key is an opaque coarse identifier, never a lat/long pair, so a
  database leak cannot be replayed into coordinates.

2.8 Out of scope

- Forecasts. This is a mood light, not a weather app.
- Any UI that names the partner's city or timezone in text.
- Severe-weather alerting. That is the safety-service line the product does
  not cross.

2.9 Open questions

- Staleness window before the sky reverts to neutral. Long enough that a
  phone in a pocket overnight does not blank the stage; short enough that a
  week-old sky never shows.
- Does daylight follow the viewer's own night too, or only the partner's?
- Weather provider, and whether it can be queried without sending anything
  more precise than a locality.


3. SOMETHING WAITING

3.1 Summary

When a status expires and a partner's half of the stage goes to the unknown
state, the other person may place one small drawn object there. It waits for
them and is present when they return.

3.2 Problem

Every presence product treats absence as a void to be filled by nagging the
absent person. Monkey Tracker already refuses to nag, which leaves the unknown
state honest but inert — a blank page with nothing to do.

This makes the emptiest moment in the product into the one place a partner can
leave you something. It is the strongest expression of the app's position that
not sharing is a complete and acceptable answer.

3.3 User story

They have been unknown since this morning. I leave a small plant on their
empty stage. When they next open the app it is there, and the first thing the
app has told them is that someone thought about them — not that they are
overdue to post.

3.4 Behavior

- Available only while the recipient's status is expired or absent.
- One object at a time per person. Placing a new one replaces the old one.
- Objects come from the existing drawn decor set. No text, no photos, no
  captions. The gesture is the message.
- The object persists across the recipient's next update rather than
  vanishing the instant they post; it should feel received, not consumed.
- The recipient may remove it at any time, silently. The sender is never told.
- No copy anywhere may frame it as a reminder, a prompt, or a request to post.

This is the absence-time case of the general placement model in section 6,
and shares its rules.

3.5 Privacy and abuse

- Rate limited per sender per day, in the same spirit as the existing poke
  cap (base 11.5) and for the same reason: a gesture that can be repeated
  indefinitely stops being a gift and becomes pressure.
- Notification, if any, is a single quiet message on the recipient's next
  foreground, never a push. The product does not push someone to come look at
  a reminder that they have been absent.
- Nothing about placement enters analytics beyond an anonymous count.

3.6 Expiry semantics

The object is not a status and makes no claim about anyone's current state.
That must be visually unambiguous: it belongs to the scene, never to the
figure, and never appears in the status line, the precision badge, or the
timeline as an update.

3.7 Open questions

- Whether the object itself expires after some days or stays until replaced.
  Staying is warmer; expiring is more consistent with the rest of the product.
- Whether both people can hold an object for each other simultaneously.
- Whether this is reachable from the widget, which is where someone is most
  likely to notice absence in the first place.


PART B — INTERACTIVE


4. LIVE PRESENCE

4.1 Summary

Three escalating ways for two people to be tangibly present to each other in
the same moment: an ambient tell that the other person is here, a mutual
hold, and a one-way squeeze.

4.2 Problem

Nothing in the product currently rewards two people being in it at the same
time. That is the one pull that satisfies rule 1.2(b) perfectly — the reward
exists only in the overlap, so the app never has to ask anyone to come back.

4.3 User story

I open the app for no reason. My monkey's ears twitch, which means they are
looking too. I press my thumb to the screen; so do they; both phones buzz
softly for as long as we both hold. Neither of us said anything.

4.4 Behavior

Tier 1 — The tell
- While both partners have the app foregrounded, each monkey shows a small
  idle animation the other can notice: ears twitching, a glance across.
- No banner, no sound, no list, no "online" label, no timestamp.
- If you are not looking at the moment it happens, nothing is lost and
  nothing is recorded.

Tier 2 — Hold hands
- Available only while Tier 1 is true for both.
- Press and hold; if both are holding, the monkeys join hands and both
  devices give a soft continuous haptic for as long as both hold.
- Releasing decays over roughly a second rather than snapping apart. A hard
  cut reads as being let go, and a dropped packet must never say that.

Tier 3 — Squeeze
- A one-way haptic tap, available whether or not the partner is present.
- Rate limited exactly like pokes (base 11.5), and for the same reason.
- Delivered silently if the partner is not in the app; it does not push.

4.5 Privacy and consent

This is the most dangerous signal in this document and needs the strictest
handling in it.

- Presence is ephemeral and unrecorded. There is no last-seen time, no
  session history, no "active 4m ago", and no way to query when someone was
  present. Those artifacts are what turn presence into surveillance, and the
  characteristic harm — "you were online and didn't answer me" — is exactly
  the kind of relationship damage this product exists to avoid.
- The tell exists only in the live moment and is never persisted, never
  logged, and never sent to analytics.
- Tier 1 is individually revocable. A partner who turns it off appears simply
  as never-present, and the other person is not told that it was turned off.
- Presence must never be inferable from any other surface: no read receipts,
  no typing indicators, no "seen" state anywhere in the product.

4.6 Failure modes

- Latency and dropped connections must fail toward "still here", decaying
  gently, never toward an abrupt disappearance.
- A partner who backgrounds the app mid-hold ends the hold by decay, and the
  other person sees a release, not a disconnection error.
- Haptics must degrade silently on devices that lack them.

4.7 Out of scope

- Any persistent online indicator.
- Presence in the widget. A widget that shows "they are in the app right now"
  is an always-on surveillance surface, which is a different product.

4.8 Open questions

- Whether Tier 1 should be on by default. It is not location data, but it is
  presence data, and the base rule for personal signals in this product is
  off-by-default.
- Whether a hold can be initiated while only one person is present, waiting
  briefly for the other — warmer, but it edges toward a summons.


5. THE QUESTION JAR

5.1 Summary

One gentle question a day. Both partners answer. Neither answer is visible
until both have answered.

Implements the "daily question" line in base 11.10 with the mechanic that
makes it work.

5.2 Problem

The product needs a reason to open that is about curiosity rather than
monitoring, and that carries no penalty for skipping. Mutual reveal supplies
both: the incentive is wanting to know what they said, and skipping costs
nothing because nothing is lost — the question simply stays unopened.

5.3 User story

There's a question waiting. I answer it, and I still cannot see theirs. Later
they answer, and both appear at once. Neither of us could lurk.

5.4 Behavior

- One question per day from a curated bank, identical for both partners.
- An answer is committed before the partner's is revealed. Both unlock
  together.
- Exactly one question is live at a time. Unanswered questions expire at end
  of day and do not accumulate. A backlog is a to-do list, and a to-do list
  is an obligation.
- No streak, no count of answered days, no record of skipped ones, anywhere,
  ever.
- Answered pairs land in the Monkey Business timeline (base 11.6) as a normal
  entry, deletable by either author like any other.

5.5 Editorial standard for the question bank

The bank is a product surface, not filler, and needs an owner.

- Questions must be answerable in one sentence by someone having a bad day.
- Nothing that implies an expectation about the relationship, and nothing
  whose non-answer would read as a verdict on it.
- Nothing that requires disclosing location, plans, or whereabouts.
- Cultural and situational neutrality: a question must work for a couple who
  are grieving, broke, ill, or fighting, or it does not ship.

5.6 Privacy

- Answers are private couple content: 30-day retention, deletable, excluded
  from notification previews, never in analytics or AI context.
- A notification, if any, announces that a question exists — never its text
  and never the partner's answer.

5.7 Out of scope for the first version

- User-written questions. Desirable, but a freeform prompt one partner writes
  for the other is a channel that can be used to hurt someone, and it needs
  its own abuse design.
- Any archive framed as a book or keepsake. See 8.14.

5.8 Open questions

- Whether both answers unlock immediately on the second answer or at a shared
  daily moment.
- Whether a question can be skipped explicitly, and whether the partner can
  tell the difference between skipped and not-yet-answered. Recommendation:
  they cannot.


6. THE STAGE YOU CAN BOTH TOUCH

6.1 Summary

The stage becomes a shared toy rather than a display. Either partner can
place, move, and remove objects on either half — including the other's — and
compose their own update by manipulating their monkey directly instead of
filling in a form.

6.2 Problem

Two problems, one cause.

A display you check is boring the moment nothing has changed. A toy stays
interesting because the other person is the content: the thing that changed
does not have to be an update any more. This satisfies rule 1.2(a) directly.

Separately, composing an update is currently a form — chip rows for activity,
mood, availability, scene. That is a significant part of why the app feels
flat. Posting should be play.

6.3 User story

I drag the sun down their sky as a joke. I put a hat on their monkey. Later
they take the hat off and leave a mug on my desk. Nothing was announced and
neither of us posted an update.

6.4 Behavior — placement

- Objects from the drawn decor set can be placed on either half of the stage,
  moved, and removed by either partner.
- Placement syncs live when both are present, and is waiting when they next
  open otherwise.
- Removal is always silent to the other person, and is always one tap.
- A clear-all is available on your own half at any time, silently.
- Placement is rate limited per sender per day, as in 3.5.
- No push notification is ever generated by placement. The reward is finding
  it, not being summoned to it.
- Section 3 is the special case of this model while a partner is absent.

6.5 Behavior — puppet composer

- The monkey's arms can be dragged directly to pose it; the pose you make is
  the pose that posts.
- Scene props can be dragged into the scene rather than chosen from a list.
- The existing chip-based composer remains available and remains the
  accessible path. Direct manipulation must never become the only way to
  post — see 6.7.

6.6 Conflict and abuse

- Simultaneous manipulation resolves last-write-wins, with live sync so the
  loser sees the result immediately rather than fighting an invisible state.
- Objects placed on someone's half belong to that person: they can always
  remove, and removal never notifies.
- Nothing placed by a partner may obscure or alter the figure, the status
  line, the precision badge, or any expiry affordance. A toy must not be able
  to cover a privacy control.

6.7 Accessibility

Direct manipulation is a hostile primary interface for motor and vision
impairment. Every action available by dragging must also be available from
the existing list-based controls, and the stage must remain fully operable by
screen reader with placement described as text.

6.8 Expiry semantics

Placed objects are scene, never status, and follow 3.6 exactly. Nothing a
partner places may make someone appear present, active, or current.

6.9 Open questions

- Whether placement on the partner's half needs a per-couple opt-in. Some
  people will find it delightful and some will find it invasive.
- Whether objects placed by a partner persist through scene changes.
- How much of the stage is manipulable before the composition stops being a
  designed illustration and becomes a mess.


7. SIT WITH ME

7.1 Summary

A mutual, time-boxed mode in which both monkeys move onto one shared stage
doing the same activity. The divider dissolves. It ends on its own.

7.2 Problem

Couples apart already do this badly: a video call left running with nobody
talking, purely for the sense of company while working, reading, or cooking.
Presence-at-the-same-moment is the one thing the product cannot fake with an
expiring status.

7.3 User story

We are both working, four thousand miles apart. I start a session, they
accept, and for fifty minutes our monkeys are at the same desk. Neither of us
says anything. It ends by itself and neither of us has to end it.

7.4 Behavior

- Either partner proposes a session: an activity, a scene, and a duration.
- The invitation expires on its own if unanswered. It does not persist, does
  not badge, does not repeat, and its expiry is never surfaced to the sender
  as a refusal.
- On acceptance both monkeys render on a single undivided stage in the shared
  scene, each keeping their own fur, face, accessory, and mood.
- The session ends at its duration. Either person may leave early with one
  tap; leaving is silent beyond the stage returning to two halves.
- While a session is live, both statuses are implicitly current: the session
  is the update. When it ends, normal expiry rules resume.
- A gentle shared marker of elapsed time is permitted. A countdown, a
  progress bar, or anything that reads as a target is not.

7.5 Privacy and consent

- Strictly mutual. No unilateral entry, ever.
- Declining is one tap, is never counted, is never shown to the other person
  as a decline, and is indistinguishable from not having seen the invite.
- No session history beyond the standard 30-day timeline; entries deletable
  by either participant.
- No content or activity from a session enters notification previews beyond
  the invitation itself, which names only the activity.

7.6 Failure modes

- Connection loss mid-session degrades to "still notionally running" and
  resolves at the scheduled end. A partner vanishing because of a dropped
  socket reads as rejection, and the product must not manufacture that.
- Simultaneous proposals collapse into one session, not two invitations.
- A proposal to a partner whose status is unknown is allowed. Absence is not
  a reason to block an invitation, and the invitation is not a nudge to post.

7.7 Out of scope for the first version

- Audio, video, or text chat inside the session. The point is company without
  conversation.
- Sessions with anyone other than the one paired partner.
- Scheduling sessions in advance.

7.8 Open questions

- Default durations. A 50/10 pomodoro shape is tempting but imports a
  productivity frame the product has avoided.
- Whether a session can be started from a widget or a watch.
- Whether 8.2 is the better primitive underneath this feature.


8. COMPACT SPECIFICATIONS

Format: what it is, why it earns its place, and the constraint most likely to
kill it.

8.1 Widgets and complications
Extends base 11.9. Home-screen and lock-screen widgets showing the partner's
monkey, and a watch complication. Highest value per unit of effort in this
document: the illustration system is already flat, two-tone, and scales to
44px by design.
Constraint: the widget must render the unknown state as faithfully as the app
does. A widget showing a stale monkey because it failed to refresh is the
worst available violation of the expiry rule. See also 4.7.

8.2 Same scene dissolves the divider
When both partners independently choose the same scene, the stage renders as
one undivided panel. No button, no announcement — you simply notice. Likely
the primitive underneath section 7.
Constraint: must not become a coordination game. If anyone feels they should
pick a scene to match, it has become pressure.

8.3 Reaching across
When both partners are live simultaneously, the monkeys lean toward the
divider. Pure ambient delight, no mechanic. Tier 0 of section 4.
Constraint: none significant. Cheapest charm in this document.

8.4 Push-to-talk walkie
Hold to send three seconds of voice. Plays immediately if the partner is
present; otherwise waits as an object on their stage.
Constraint: audio is content. Same private-storage handling as photo
postcards, and it must never autoplay from a notification.

8.5 Banana toss
Throw it across the divider; catchable if the partner is live, landing and
waiting if not. Absurd, pointless, on-tone.
Constraint: no scores, no records, no streaks. See 9.2.

8.6 The rope
Both pull. Nobody wins. Both monkeys wobble.
Constraint: deliberately unwinnable. The moment it has an outcome it acquires
a record, and a record is a scoreboard.

8.7 Heartbeat
From a watch, sent as a haptic rhythm on the partner's phone.
Constraint: biometric data. Needs its own consent surface and must never be
stored, only transmitted live.

8.8 Blind mood match
Each partner picks a mood for the other — "how do I think you're feeling?" —
revealed together, as in section 5.
Constraint: being guessed wrong repeatedly can land badly. No history, no
accuracy score, ever.

8.9 Scratch-off postcards
A photo arrives obscured; rubbing reveals it. Trivial interaction,
disproportionate delight.
Constraint: reveal must be skippable. A gesture gate on content someone
wanted to see becomes friction the second time.

8.10 Doodle replies and doodle telephone
A small drawing that appears as a prop on the partner's stage for an hour, in
the illustration system's ink weight; optionally as a guessing game.
Constraint: freeform user content needs a moderation story even at a scale of
two people, because two people can still hurt each other.

8.11 Build the treehouse
A persistent shared structure each partner adds pieces to. No timer, no
decay, no counter. This is base 11.11's terrarium done correctly, and it is
the recommended resolution of the conflict in 9.3: pieces accrue from shared
moments both people took part in, never from individual posting frequency.
Constraint: the moment progress can be lost, it is a streak.

8.12 Co-drawing on the sky
When both are live, both scribble in the same sky; it fades over an hour.
Constraint: as 8.10, plus a hard rule that it cannot obscure status or
privacy affordances (6.6).

8.13 The evening card
At day's end, one illustrated card showing both days side by side. Nothing to
do, just something to look at.
Constraint: must be generated from what already exists. If it can be
"incomplete", it is a chore.

8.14 The monthly spread
Extends the base PRD's P2 "Monkey Recap". An illustrated printable poster
from saved moments — also the honest way to keep something past the 30-day
retention window.
Constraint: generated only from data the user already chose to save, never
from a re-scan of expired content.

8.15 Trail as a walk, not a map
Replace any map surface for Trail with the monkey walking across the stage,
arriving somewhere at the end. Motion without coordinates.
Constraint: most likely feature to be misread as a safety service. Copy must
be unambiguous that it is a feeling, not a fix.

8.16 Overlap light
A band of light showing when the two people's waking hours intersect.
Depends on 2.4 Tier 1.
Constraint: must not become a scheduling tool, or it acquires obligations.

8.17 Zero-friction input
NFC stickers, shortcuts, and widget taps that post a preset scene in one
gesture.
Constraint: presets must not drift into automation. An update the user did
not consciously make is a location beacon wearing a costume.

8.18 Postcard as backdrop
An existing private postcard becomes the soft backdrop of that person's half
of the stage.
Constraint: a backdrop is far more visible than a thumbnail, including to
anyone glancing at the phone over a shoulder.

8.19 Ambient sound
Five seconds of ambient audio attached to an update, expiring with it.
Constraint: as 8.4.

8.20 Ask for Perch
A partner may request a precision upgrade for a bounded window; approve or
decline in one tap.
Constraint: the app must never surface how often anyone declined, to either
party. Cheap AND invisible refusal is the whole feature; a decline count
turns the consent model into a scoreboard.

8.21 Fade styles
Choose how an update expires: straight to unknown, or leaving a residue —
the mug stays after you have left the café.
Constraint: residue must be visually incapable of reading as current status.
If it can be mistaken for presence, it is cut.

8.22 Letters on a state
Write something delivered when the partner is next Free, or next Sleepy.
Constraint: delivery conditions must not create an incentive to set a
particular state. A letter waiting behind "Free" is a reason to lie about
being free.

8.23 The goodnight ritual
Extends base 11.10. Both set Asleep; the stage goes to night together and one
light stays on for whoever wakes first.
Constraint: "who went to bed first" must never become legible as a pattern.

8.24 The monkey frame
A small e-ink desk object showing the partner's current monkey. The
illustration system would render on e-ink with no redesign.
Constraint: a physical always-on display of someone's presence in a shared
room is a different consent question than a phone screen, and needs its own.


9. REJECTED, AND ONE CONFLICT

9.1 Mood-driven weather — rejected
Combining both partners' moods into the stage's weather quietly makes one
person's bad day into the thing that ruined the picture. That is guilt with
extra steps. If mood ever drives the sky, it drives only your own half, and
never darkens it.

9.2 Anything that counts — rejected as a category
Unlocks, wardrobes, collections, records, and accuracy scores all drift
toward streaks. If the treehouse fills up over time it fills from variety or
from shared moments, never from consistency, and it never shows a counter.

9.3 Online status and last-seen — rejected, permanently
Presence in section 4 is deliberately unrecorded. Any last-seen timestamp,
session history, read receipt, typing indicator, or "active 4m ago" is
rejected outright and not subject to later reconsideration. The harm is
specific and well known — "you were online and didn't answer me" — and it is
precisely the relationship damage this product exists to avoid.

9.4 Return notifications — rejected as a category
No notification may exist whose purpose is to bring someone back to the app.
Notifications announce that something happened; they never ask for a visit,
and they never reference how long it has been.

9.5 Surprise deliveries — accepted only under two conditions
Rarely and randomly, a firefly or a visiting bird appears on the stage. This
is a variable-ratio reward, which is the slot-machine mechanic, and it would
normally be rejected under 9.2. It is defensible only if both hold: it is not
contingent on anything the user did, and it is never notified. That makes it
a reward for a visit already being made rather than a machine for
manufacturing visits. If it ever acquires a push notification, it is cut.

9.6 Conflict in the base PRD
Base 11.11 "Couple terrarium" specifies that users "earn bananas for posting,
reacting, or completing rituals". Earning a currency for posting is a
participation incentive — the mechanism the product's own rules forbid. The
existing guard that missing a day "does not destroy progress" limits the
damage without removing the incentive. Recommended resolution: bananas accrue
from shared moments both people took part in, never from individual posting
frequency. See 8.11.


10. SEQUENCING

1. Reaching across (8.3) and same scene dissolves the divider (8.2). Two
   days of work, disproportionate charm, and 8.2 is groundwork for 7.
2. Live presence Tier 1, the tell (4.4). The first thing that makes the app
   feel like there is a person on the other end rather than a database. No
   new permissions, no notifications, nothing recorded.
3. Local Sky Tier 1 (2.4). Smallest of the ambient features, needs no
   permission, uses the stage that already exists, and changes how the app
   feels on first open.
4. Widgets (8.1). Highest value per unit effort, and it puts the sky and the
   monkey where people will actually see them.
5. The question jar (5). Independent of everything above; the strongest
   non-live reason to open. Needs the editorial standard in 5.5 owned first.
6. Something waiting (3). The strongest statement of what this product
   believes, and the natural precursor to the general placement model.
7. Live presence Tiers 2 and 3 (4.4). Needs live transport and haptics.
8. The stage you can both touch (6). Large: live sync, conflict resolution,
   an accessibility path, and a new composer.
9. Local Sky Tier 2 (weather). Needs the coarse-locality consent surface
   designed properly; do not rush it behind Tier 1.
10. Sit with me (7). The largest build: live presence, mutual invitation,
    session state. Worth it, but last.

Note the shape: everything through step 5 is achievable without live
transport. Steps 7, 8, and 10 all depend on it, so the realtime presence
channel is the single largest architectural commitment in this document and
should be designed once, deliberately, before step 7.


11. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT ANSWER

- Whether any of this changes the Release 0 constraint of exactly one couple.
  Nothing here requires more than two people, and nothing here should be used
  as an argument for relaxing that.
- Cost and load. Weather lookups, live presence, live placement sync, and
  sessions all add backend load the current single-project Supabase setup has
  never been sized for. Live presence in particular is a persistent
  connection per user, which is a different cost model than expiring rows.
- Native platform work. Widgets, complications, haptics, NFC, watch, and
  e-ink are all outside the Expo-managed surface the app currently lives in.
- Moderation. Several Part B features introduce freeform content between two
  people. Two people can still hurt each other, and no feature in 8.10, 8.12,
  or 5.7 should ship without deciding what happens when one does.
