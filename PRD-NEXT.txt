MONKEY TRACKER
Product Requirements Document — Release 1 candidates

Version: 0.3
Status: Draft for review. Nothing here is committed.
Extends: PRD.txt v1.0. Section numbers below are local to this document;
         cross-references to the base PRD are written as "base 11.x".
Informed by: competitive research readout, 2026. Findings are summarised in
         1.2 and carried as constraints throughout.


0. HOW TO READ THIS

Release 0 shipped presence: one couple, expiring updates, optional location,
an illustrated stage. It works, and it is boring. Every feature here answers
the same complaint — that opening the app when nothing has changed gives you
nothing — without reaching for the two things the product has already
refused: pressure to post, and precision about where someone is.

The strategy is not novelty. This is a well-populated category and the plan is
to build the well-made version of it. Section 1.2 records what the category
has already proved and what it has already killed; those findings are binding
constraints in the sections that follow, not background reading.

Structure:

  Section 1    Position, rules, and the two tests every feature must pass.
  Section 2    Risks. Placed early because they determine sequencing.
  Part A (3-5) Ambient: reasons to open when nobody has done anything.
  Part B (6-10) Interactive: reasons to open now.
  Part C (11-12) The long game: what survives week three, and how anyone
               finds out this exists.
  Section 13   Compact specifications.
  Section 14   Rejected, and one conflict in the base PRD.
  Section 15   Sequencing.
  Section 16   What this document does not answer.

Every feature must satisfy the base PRD's standing rules:

- Optional and off by default if it touches location in any form.
- No guilt, no punishment, no streaks, no shaming a partner for not sharing.
- Expired means unknown. Never imply a stale state is current.
- No exact location, private captions, or sensitive status content in
  analytics, logs, notification previews, or AI context.
- Not an emergency, safety-monitoring, or proof-of-location service.

A feature that cannot satisfy those is cut, not negotiated.


1. POSITION, RULES, AND TESTS

1.1 The goal

Make the app worth opening when your partner has not just posted.

Part A adds change nobody has to author. Part B gives two people things that
are only possible together, or that are simply fun to do. Part C asks what is
still true in month six.

1.2 What the category has already proved

Recorded here because these findings constrain specific sections below.

Craft alone is a viable strategy. Zenly was Find My with better animation and
reached roughly 40M monthly actives on execution, not invention; it died of
acquisition politics rather than product failure. Locket did not invent the
photo widget and reached roughly 80M downloads and profitability at fifteen
people with no streak mechanics anywhere. The bar for "the nicely made one"
in this category is low, and the incumbents are not defending it: Widgetable
is winning on mechanics while ad-choked, and Between is fourteen years old, on
its fourth owner, and recently deleted users' photos.

The warning is sharper than the precedent. Jelly (China, 2022) and Bondee
(SEA, 2023) both shipped avatar-performs-your-status, both hit #1, and both
died. They did not die of being unoriginal. They died of not being fun after
about three weeks, because their fun was front-loaded: character creation was
one large hit followed by a status menu. This is the closest prior art to
Monkey Tracker that exists, and it failed for a reason that applies directly.

Three consequences run through this document:

- The empty stage is the central product problem, not a polish item. With one
  content source, there is nothing to see when the partner is asleep.
- Combination states and partner-generated content are the two mechanics the
  category has left on the table.
- Archive, not delight, is what held users for a decade. See section 11.

1.3 The two tests

Test one — the pull must be honest.

"Make people want to open the app" is ordinary product language that, applied
literally, produces streaks, nudge notifications, counters, badges, and
variable rewards for posting. Every one of those is forbidden above, and the
ban is not a technicality: a couples app that manufactures compulsion is
actively harmful to the relationship it claims to serve.

The pull may come from exactly three places:

  (a) Someone touched my world. The thing that changed was done by the one
      person I care about, not by a scheduler.
  (b) This is only possible right now. The reward exists in the overlap of
      two people being present, so the app never has to ask for the visit.
  (c) This is fun to do. The interaction is its own reason.

Obligation may not become a fourth.

Test two — the fun must be renewable.

Fun we ship runs out. Fun the couple manufactures does not. For every feature,
ask where the next instance of it comes from:

- Front-loaded: avatar colorways, accessory sets, scene variety, any finite
  library of authored assets. Monkey Tracker has already shipped most of its
  front-loaded fun. It should not be mistaken for retention.
- Renewable: combination states, placement, live presence, anything whose
  content is generated by the other person.

An honest example of the distinction: the question jar (section 8) is a
genuinely good mechanic whose supply is a finite bank we author. It is
front-loaded fun wearing a renewable coat, and section 8.5 says so.

1.4 The differentiator to lead with

Graduated location precision — hidden, a named place, a rough area, a live
trail — off by default, expiring to genuinely unknown, where declining to
share looks identical to simply not having posted.

Nothing in the researched landscape does this. The invisible decline is the
part that matters and the part that is hard to copy: Life360 structurally
cannot follow, because a large share of its revenue is advertising and data.

This is already specified three times in this document (6.5, 7.5, 13.16) and
positioned nowhere. It should lead the product's public description, the store
listing, and the first-run explanation.


2. RISKS ON THE BOARD

2.1 Front-loaded fun (highest)
The closest prior art died of this at three weeks. Mitigations are sections
3, 5, 6 and 9 — every one of which exists to make the content come from the
couple rather than from us. Test two in 1.3 is the standing check.

2.2 The empty stage
One content source means nothing to see when the partner is asleep or absent.
Sections 3 and 4 are the direct mitigation, and they are sequenced first for
that reason.

2.3 Battery
Jelly users reported drain worse than TikTok. Live location plus continuous
animation is the same profile, and this document proposes both. Section 3.7
sets an animation budget; section 15 defers live transport as far as it can be
deferred. Any feature that adds a persistent connection needs a measured
battery number before it ships, not after.

2.4 No network effect
Two-person by design means a viral coefficient near 1.0. Growth is paid
acquisition or a shareable moment, and the product currently produces no
shareable artifact at all. Section 12 addresses this and is the least
resolved section in the document.

2.5 Cost model
Every live feature is a persistent connection per user, which is a different
cost shape from expiring rows. Locket reached profitability at fifteen people;
that is the shape to aim at, and it argues against building the expensive
realtime channel early.

2.6 Retention beyond delight
Novelty gets a couple to week three. Section 11 is the answer to month six,
and it is in tension with the 30-day retention rule.


PART A — AMBIENT


3. IDLE LIFE

3.1 Summary

The monkey does something when nobody has posted. It reacts to inputs that
change on their own — time of day, the partner's local night, time since last
contact — so the stage is never a still image.

3.2 Problem

This is the direct answer to 2.1 and 2.2, and the single most important
feature in this document.

Jelly's avatar moved only when you set a status; between updates it was
furniture. Monkey Tracker is currently in the same position: the illustration
system is expressive, and it is static until someone posts. With one content
source, a partner asleep in another timezone means an app with nothing in it.

Sky: Children of the Light keeps people opening it because the world changes
independently of anyone's friends. The same trick is available here at a tiny
fraction of the cost, because the figure already has five eye shapes, five
mouth shapes, a pose layer, and an accessory layer that currently only move
when an update changes.

3.3 User story

I open the app at lunch. Nobody has posted since this morning. My partner's
monkey is dozing because it is 2am where they are, and mine is squinting in
the afternoon light. Nothing has been reported and there is still something
to look at.

3.4 Behavior

Idle motion is driven only by inputs that change without anyone acting:

- Local time of day for that person (timezone only — see 3.5).
- The partner's local night, which puts their monkey to sleep.
- Time elapsed since that person's last update.
- Weather, only if that person has opted into 4.4 Tier 2.

Idle motion is small and continuous: breathing, blinking, an occasional ear
twitch, a shift of weight, a glance. It reuses the existing eye, mouth and
pose layers rather than adding an animation system.

Hard constraint: idle motion may never be contingent on posting frequency,
recency of the viewer's own activity, or anything the user could increase by
using the app more. An avatar that gets livelier the more you post is a streak
with fur on it, and is rejected under 14.2.

3.5 Privacy

Idle life must work with zero location data. Time of day comes from the
timezone offset already covered by 4.5 Tier 1 consent; if that is off, the
monkey idles on the viewer's own clock instead and nothing is revealed.

Weather is a coarse-locality consent surface (4.5 Tier 2) and does not become
a dependency of this feature. Idle life must not be the reason someone turns
on location sharing.

3.6 Expiry semantics

This is the subtle risk in the feature. A moving monkey reads as a present
person, and an expired status must never read as presence.

- An unknown monkey still idles, but idles in the unknown treatment: the
  blank-page figure of the existing design, with slower motion, no activity
  props, and no accessory.
- Idle motion may never be interpreted as, or described as, activity. No copy
  anywhere may say the partner is "around", "active", or "awake".
- Sleeping-because-it-is-night is derived from the clock, not from a status,
  and must be visually distinct from the unknown state, which is derived from
  expiry. Two different silences that look the same would be a lie about one
  of them.

3.7 Battery budget

Per 2.3, this feature is the main always-on cost in the product.

- Animation runs only while the app is foregrounded. No background animation,
  ever.
- Idle motion pauses when the device reports low power mode.
- A frame budget and a measured battery-per-hour figure are required before
  ship, not after.
- Widgets render a still frame. See 13.1.

3.8 Out of scope

- Idle motion driven by the viewer's own behaviour in the app.
- Any idle behaviour that reacts to how long it has been since the partner
  posted in a way the partner could read as impatience. See 5.6.

3.9 Open questions

- Whether idle differs per activity, or is one shared vocabulary.
- Whether the monkey should visibly sleep, or simply settle. Sleeping is
  charming and also states something about the partner's night that the
  timezone consent may not have intended to state.
- How much motion is too much on a stage someone glances at forty times a day.


4. LOCAL SKY

4.1 Summary

Each half of the stage runs on its partner's own local clock, and optionally
their own weather. Their side goes amber at their sunset and dark at their
night. Rain streaks their sky when it is raining on them.

4.2 Problem

For a long-distance couple in different timezones, the single most useful
fact — "is it a reasonable hour for them right now?" — is currently absent
from a product whose entire job is answering "what is my person up to?".

Second, with section 3, it is half of the answer to the empty stage: the
figure moves and the world behind it moves, on different clocks.

4.3 User story

As someone twelve hours out of sync with my partner, I open the app, see that
their side is dark and quiet, and decide not to call — without reading a
number, doing arithmetic, or being told what to do.

4.4 Behavior

Tier 1 — Daylight
- Derived from the partner's device timezone offset only.
- Drives sky colour, sun and moon position, and star field across four bands:
  dawn, day, dusk, night.
- Transitions are continuous, not stepped, so the stage drifts over a day.
- No location permission is required or requested.
- Feeds section 3's idle inputs.

Tier 2 — Weather
- Derived from a coarse locality, city-level or coarser, never coordinates.
- Adds one weather layer: clear, cloud, rain, snow, fog.
- Requires its own explicit opt-in, separate from Tier 1 and separate from the
  existing location precision control.

4.5 Privacy

- A timezone offset is not a coordinate, but it narrows longitude to a band
  and, for a couple who know roughly where each other live, reveals travel.
  Tier 1 is therefore a consent surface: off by default, revocable in one tap.
- Tier 2 is location data. It obeys every rule the existing precision control
  obeys: off by default, plainly labeled, never required for an update, never
  resolved finer than a locality.
- Neither tier may be inferred from an IP address.
- Turning either tier off takes effect promptly and must not notify the
  partner. Silent revocation is the point; a "your partner stopped sharing
  daylight" notice converts a privacy control into a social cost.
- Weather and daylight are excluded from notification previews. "It is raining
  on your partner" is a location claim in a lock-screen banner.

4.6 Expiry semantics

Daylight and weather are properties of the person, not of an update.

- They continue to render after a status expires. An unknown status does not
  black out the sky.
- If the ambient signal goes stale (window per 4.9), the sky returns to a
  neutral field. It does not freeze on the last known sky, which would be the
  same category of lie as a stale status.

4.7 Data model

- A small record per troop member: tz offset, optional locality key, optional
  weather code, freshness timestamp. Written on foreground and a low-frequency
  refresh, not per update.
- Locality key is an opaque coarse identifier, never a lat/long pair, so a
  database leak cannot be replayed into coordinates.

4.8 Out of scope

- Forecasts. This is a mood light, not a weather app.
- Any UI naming the partner's city or timezone in text.
- Severe-weather alerting. That is the safety-service line.

4.9 Open questions

- Staleness window before the sky reverts to neutral.
- Does daylight follow the viewer's own night too, or only the partner's?
- A weather provider that can be queried with nothing finer than a locality.


5. COMBINATION STATES

5.1 Summary

The two panels stop being two independent pictures. What each monkey does
depends on what the other is doing.

5.2 Problem

The category has not exploited pairing. Every avatar app in the research
renders one avatar per person and stops there, which means the second panel
adds information but no delight.

Combination states are the cheapest renewable fun available to this product,
per test two in 1.3: the content is generated by the couple's own combination
of choices, so it does not run out the way an authored asset library does.
They are also the reason to look at a partner's update rather than merely
read it.

5.3 User story

We both happen to be eating. I open the app and our monkeys are eating
together, at the same table, in one undivided scene. Neither of us arranged
that and neither of us was told it would happen.

5.4 Behavior

Combination states are emergent. They are never announced, never named in
copy, never listed in a UI, and never suggested.

The matrix to start with:

- Same scene chosen by both: the divider dissolves and the stage renders as
  one undivided panel.
- Same activity chosen by both: the monkeys perform it together rather than
  side by side.
- Both live in the app at once: they lean toward the divider. See section 7.
- One in local night, one in local day: the daylit side is rendered normally
  and the night side settles, without either monkey commenting on the other.
- Both unknown: the stage is quiet and neutral. Not sad, not empty-feeling —
  the existing blank-page treatment, on both sides.

5.5 The guilt trap

This is the constraint most likely to kill a specific state, and it must be
checked for every state added later.

No combination state may dramatise absence. A present monkey that looks at the
empty half, waits by the divider, droops, or otherwise performs missing its
partner converts the other person's silence into a visible reproach. That is
the exact harm the product's rules exist to prevent, and it is easy to build
by accident because it is genuinely poignant.

The rule: when one side is unknown, the other side behaves as though nothing
is wrong.

5.6 Non-coercion

No combination state may create an incentive to match. If a user ever feels
they should pick a scene so that the divider dissolves, the mechanic has
become pressure and the state is cut. In practice this means: no state may be
discoverable as a goal, and nothing in the product may hint that a particular
pairing produces a particular result.

5.7 Expiry semantics

A combination requires both statuses to be current. When one expires, the
combination ends immediately and that side falls back to unknown. A pair state
that outlives one of its participants would be a stale-state lie of the worst
kind, because it implies presence the product knows is gone.

5.8 Open questions

- How large the matrix should get before authoring cost outruns the delight,
  and whether combinations should compose (same scene AND same activity) or
  be selected by priority.
- Whether combination states should exist for accessory or mood pairings, or
  only activity and scene.
- Whether any combination should be visible in the widget. Probably not: a
  widget cannot show the moment it happened.


PART B — INTERACTIVE


6. PLACEMENT

6.1 Summary

Either partner can leave a small drawn object on the other's half of the
stage. It stays until removed. Removal is always silent.

6.2 Problem

The research is unambiguous that letting each person modify the other's side
is the most validated intimacy mechanic in the category — it is the whole of
noteit's business and the core of Widgetable's pet gifting — and that no
avatar app has done it well.

It also satisfies both tests in 1.3 outright: the pull is 1.3(a), and the fun
is renewable because a person generates it.

Its most important case is absence. When a status expires and a partner's half
goes to the unknown state, every other presence product treats that void as a
reason to nag the absent person. Placement makes it the one place a partner
can leave you something instead.

6.3 User story

They have been unknown since this morning. I leave a small plant on their
empty stage. When they next open the app it is there, and the first thing the
app has told them is that someone thought about them — not that they are
overdue to post.

6.4 Behavior

- Objects come from the drawn decor set. No text, no photos, no captions. The
  gesture is the message.
- Placement works whether the recipient is current or unknown.
- Objects persist across the recipient's next update rather than vanishing the
  moment they post. They should feel received, not consumed.
- The recipient may remove any object at any time, silently and in one tap,
  and may clear their own half entirely. The sender is never told.
- No push notification is ever generated by placement. The reward is finding
  it, not being summoned to it.
- Rate limited per sender per day, in the spirit of the existing poke cap
  (base 11.5) and for the same reason: a gesture that can be repeated without
  limit stops being a gift and becomes pressure.
- No copy anywhere may frame a placed object as a reminder, a prompt, or a
  request to post.

Section 9 extends this from placement to live manipulation. This section is
deliberately buildable without live transport.

6.5 Privacy

- Nothing about placement enters analytics beyond an anonymous count.
- A notification, if any, is a single quiet message on next foreground, never
  a push, and never one that references how long the recipient has been away.

6.6 Expiry semantics

A placed object is not a status and makes no claim about anyone's state. It
belongs to the scene, never to the figure, and never appears in the status
line, the precision badge, or the timeline as an update. Nothing a partner
places may make someone appear present, active, or current.

6.7 Constraints on what can be placed

Nothing placed by a partner may obscure or alter the figure, the status line,
the precision badge, or any expiry affordance. A toy must not be able to cover
a privacy control.

6.8 Open questions

- Whether one object at a time, or several, and whether a cap is per sender
  or per stage.
- Whether objects expire on their own after some days. Staying is warmer;
  expiring is more consistent with the rest of the product.
- Whether placement should require a per-couple opt-in. Some people will find
  it delightful and some will find it invasive.


7. LIVE PRESENCE

7.1 Summary

Three escalating ways for two people to be tangibly present in the same
moment: an ambient tell that the other person is here, a mutual hold, and a
one-way squeeze.

7.2 Problem

Nothing in the product rewards two people being in it at the same time. That
is the one pull that satisfies 1.3(b) perfectly — the reward exists only in
the overlap, so the app never has to ask anyone to come back.

7.3 User story

I open the app for no reason. My monkey's ears twitch, which means they are
looking too. I press my thumb to the screen; so do they; both phones buzz
softly for as long as we both hold. Neither of us said anything.

7.4 Behavior

Tier 1 — The tell
- While both partners have the app foregrounded, each monkey shows a small
  idle variation the other can notice.
- No banner, no sound, no list, no "online" label, no timestamp.
- If you are not looking when it happens, nothing is lost and nothing is
  recorded.

Tier 2 — Hold hands
- Available only while Tier 1 is true for both.
- Press and hold; if both hold, the monkeys join hands and both devices give a
  soft continuous haptic for as long as both hold.
- Release decays over roughly a second rather than snapping apart. A hard cut
  reads as being let go, and a dropped packet must never say that.

Tier 3 — Squeeze
- A one-way haptic tap, available whether or not the partner is present.
- Rate limited exactly like pokes (base 11.5).
- Delivered silently if the partner is not in the app. It does not push.

7.5 Privacy and consent

The most dangerous signal in this document, and it gets the strictest handling
in it.

- Presence is ephemeral and unrecorded. No last-seen time, no session history,
  no "active 4m ago", no way to query when someone was present. Those
  artifacts are what turn presence into surveillance, and the characteristic
  harm — "you were online and didn't answer me" — is exactly the relationship
  damage this product exists to avoid.
- The tell exists only in the live moment. Never persisted, never logged,
  never sent to analytics.
- Tier 1 is individually revocable. A partner who turns it off appears simply
  as never-present, and the other person is not told.
- Presence must never be inferable from any other surface: no read receipts,
  no typing indicators, no "seen" state anywhere in the product.

7.6 Failure modes

- Latency and dropped connections fail toward "still here", decaying gently,
  never toward abrupt disappearance.
- Backgrounding mid-hold ends the hold by decay; the other person sees a
  release, not an error.
- Haptics degrade silently on devices that lack them.

7.7 Cost and battery

This section introduces the persistent connection that 2.3 and 2.5 warn about.
It must not ship without a measured battery figure and a per-user connection
cost model, and it is sequenced accordingly in section 15.

7.8 Out of scope

- Any persistent online indicator.
- Presence in the widget. A widget showing "they are in the app right now" is
  an always-on surveillance surface, which is a different product.

7.9 Open questions

- Whether Tier 1 is on by default. It is not location data, but it is presence
  data, and the base rule for personal signals here is off-by-default.
- Whether a hold can be initiated by one person, waiting briefly for the
  other — warmer, but it edges toward a summons.


8. THE QUESTION JAR

8.1 Summary

One gentle question a day. Both partners answer. Neither answer is visible
until both have answered.

Implements the "daily question" line in base 11.10 with the mechanic that
makes it work.

8.2 Problem

The product needs a reason to open that is about curiosity rather than
monitoring, and that carries no penalty for skipping. Mutual reveal supplies
both: the incentive is wanting to know what they said, and skipping costs
nothing, because the question simply stays unopened.

8.3 User story

There's a question waiting. I answer it, and I still cannot see theirs. Later
they answer, and both appear at once. Neither of us could lurk.

8.4 Behavior

- One question per day from a curated bank, identical for both partners.
- An answer is committed before the partner's is revealed. Both unlock
  together.
- Exactly one question is live at a time. Unanswered questions expire at end
  of day and do not accumulate. A backlog is a to-do list, and a to-do list is
  an obligation.
- No streak, no count of answered days, no record of skipped ones, anywhere.
- Answered pairs land in the Monkey Business timeline (base 11.6) as a normal
  entry, deletable by either author.

8.5 Honest note on renewability

Per test two in 1.3, this feature's supply is a bank we author. It is
front-loaded fun in a renewable-looking wrapper, and it will run out on a
schedule we can calculate from bank size divided by one per day.

Two mitigations, neither free: a bank large enough that exhaustion is years
away, or user-written questions, which 8.8 puts out of scope for the first
version because a freeform prompt one partner writes for the other is a
channel that can be used to hurt someone.

This does not disqualify the feature. It does mean it cannot be the answer to
2.1, and it should not be sequenced as though it were.

8.6 Editorial standard for the bank

The bank is a product surface, not filler, and needs an owner.

- Answerable in one sentence by someone having a bad day.
- Nothing implying an expectation about the relationship, and nothing whose
  non-answer would read as a verdict on it.
- Nothing requiring disclosure of location, plans, or whereabouts.
- A question must work for a couple who are grieving, broke, ill, or
  fighting, or it does not ship.

8.7 Privacy

- Answers are private couple content: 30-day retention unless saved under
  section 11, deletable, excluded from notification previews, never in
  analytics or AI context.
- A notification, if any, announces that a question exists — never its text
  and never the partner's answer.

8.8 Out of scope for the first version

- User-written questions. See 8.5.
- Any archive framed as a keepsake. That is section 11's job.

8.9 Open questions

- Whether both answers unlock immediately on the second answer, or at a shared
  daily moment.
- Whether skipping is explicit, and whether the partner can distinguish
  skipped from not-yet-answered. Recommendation: they cannot.


9. THE STAGE YOU CAN BOTH TOUCH

9.1 Summary

Placement (section 6) extended into live manipulation, plus a composer that
works by handling the monkey directly instead of filling in a form.

9.2 Problem

Two problems, one cause.

A display you check is boring the moment nothing has changed. A toy stays
interesting because the other person is the content. This is the fullest
expression of 1.3(a) and of renewable fun.

Separately, composing an update is currently a form — chip rows for activity,
mood, availability, scene. That is a significant part of why the app feels
flat, and it is also front-loaded fun by construction: a list of options is
exactly the shape of thing that runs out.

9.3 User story

I drag the sun down their sky as a joke. I put a hat on their monkey. Later
they take the hat off and leave a mug on my desk. Nothing was announced and
neither of us posted an update.

9.4 Behavior — live manipulation

- Objects can be placed, moved, and removed on either half, syncing live when
  both are present and waiting when they are not.
- All of section 6's rules apply unchanged, including silent removal, rate
  limits, no pushes, and 6.7's constraint on covering controls.
- Simultaneous manipulation resolves last-write-wins with live sync, so the
  loser sees the result immediately rather than fighting invisible state.

9.5 Behavior — puppet composer

- The monkey's arms can be dragged to pose it; the pose you make is the pose
  that posts.
- Scene props can be dragged in rather than chosen from a list.
- The existing chip composer remains available and remains the accessible
  path. Direct manipulation must never become the only way to post.

9.6 Accessibility

Direct manipulation is a hostile primary interface for motor and vision
impairment. Every action available by dragging must also be available from
list-based controls, and the stage must remain fully operable by screen reader
with placement described as text. This is a ship blocker, not a follow-up.

9.7 Expiry semantics

As 6.6. Nothing a partner places or moves may make someone appear present,
active, or current.

9.8 Open questions

- Whether objects placed by a partner persist through scene changes.
- How much of the stage is manipulable before the composition stops being a
  designed illustration and becomes a mess.
- Whether the puppet composer can express the full domain model, or only a
  subset with the chip composer as the complete path.


10. SIT WITH ME

10.1 Summary

A mutual, time-boxed mode in which both monkeys move onto one shared stage
doing the same activity. The divider dissolves. It ends on its own.

10.2 Problem

Couples apart already do this badly: a video call left running with nobody
talking, purely for company while working, reading, or cooking.
Presence-at-the-same-moment is the one thing the product cannot fake with an
expiring status.

10.3 User story

We are both working, four thousand miles apart. I start a session, they
accept, and for fifty minutes our monkeys are at the same desk. Neither of us
says anything. It ends by itself and neither of us has to end it.

10.4 Behavior

- Either partner proposes a session: activity, scene, duration.
- The invitation expires on its own if unanswered. It does not persist, badge,
  or repeat, and its expiry is never surfaced to the sender as a refusal.
- On acceptance both monkeys render on one undivided stage, each keeping their
  own fur, face, accessory, and mood.
- The session ends at its duration. Either person may leave early with one
  tap; leaving is silent beyond the stage returning to two halves.
- While live, both statuses are implicitly current: the session is the update.
  Normal expiry rules resume when it ends.
- A gentle shared marker of elapsed time is permitted. A countdown, a progress
  bar, or anything that reads as a target is not.

10.5 Privacy and consent

- Strictly mutual. No unilateral entry, ever.
- Declining is one tap, never counted, never shown as a decline, and
  indistinguishable from not having seen the invitation.
- No session history beyond the standard timeline; entries deletable by either
  participant.
- No content from a session enters notification previews beyond the invitation
  itself, which names only the activity.

10.6 Failure modes

- Connection loss degrades to "still notionally running" and resolves at the
  scheduled end. A partner vanishing because of a dropped socket reads as
  rejection, and the product must not manufacture that.
- Simultaneous proposals collapse into one session.
- A proposal to a partner whose status is unknown is allowed. Absence is not a
  reason to block an invitation, and the invitation is not a nudge to post.

10.7 Out of scope for the first version

- Audio, video, or text chat. The point is company without conversation.
- Sessions with anyone but the one paired partner.
- Scheduling in advance.

10.8 Open questions

- Default durations. A 50/10 pomodoro shape imports a productivity frame the
  product has avoided.
- Whether a session can start from a widget or a watch.
- Whether section 5's same-scene state is the better primitive underneath this.


PART C — THE LONG GAME


11. ACCUMULATION WITHOUT A COUNTER

11.1 Summary

The couple accumulates something that is neither state nor score: a stage that
furnishes over time and moments they chose to keep.

11.2 Problem

Novelty gets a couple to week three; sections 3, 5, 6 and 9 exist to get them
there. Almost nothing in this document gets them to month six.

The research finding is blunt: Between held users for a decade on archive, not
delight, and the clearest signal in the entire landscape was its users' rage
when their photos were deleted. What people stay for is the record.

Monkey Tracker is built on expiry, which is its best idea and is not up for
negotiation. The two are reconcilable only if the thing that accumulates is
not status.

11.3 The tension with retention, stated plainly

The base PRD sets 30-day retention as a privacy decision. This section reveals
that it is also, accidentally, a strategic one: a product that remembers
nothing gives a couple nothing to lose, and nothing to lose is also nothing to
stay for.

That does not mean retention should be extended. It means the decision must be
made deliberately, with both consequences visible, rather than inherited. The
recommended resolution is that the default remains 30 days and anything that
outlives it does so only because a person explicitly kept it.

11.4 Behavior

Two mechanisms, both deliberately counter-free:

Saved moments
- A timeline entry can be kept beyond retention by explicit action.
- Kept items are per-item, deletable at any time, and included in export and
  deletion flows.
- No count of saved items is displayed anywhere.

A stage that furnishes
- The stage accrues small permanent details from shared moments both people
  took part in — a session sat together, a question both answered, a scene
  both chose on the same day.
- Nothing accrues from one person's activity alone.
- Nothing can ever be lost, decay, or be taken away.
- No counter, level, progress bar, or completion state exists anywhere.

11.5 The "monkeys learn" trap

An avatar that develops over time is a lovely idea and a streak in a costume
if it develops from frequency. The line is exact:

- Permitted: development from shared moments, from variety, from elapsed
  relationship time — things a couple cannot fail at.
- Rejected: development from posting frequency, session counts, consecutive
  days, or anything a user could increase by opening the app more.

This is the same failure the base PRD already contains at 11.11, and the same
resolution. See 14.6.

11.6 Privacy

Anything surviving the retention window is a deliberate exception to it and
must be: chosen per item by a person, visible in a single place, deletable
individually, and covered by the deletion and export flows the base PRD
requires before wider testing.

11.7 Open questions

- What happens to accumulated content when a couple unpairs. This is the
  hardest human question in the document: a furnished stage and a kept archive
  are exactly what someone would most want to take, and most want deleted,
  after a breakup. It needs an answer designed on purpose, and the answer
  probably differs for the two cases.
- Whether furnishing is visible to both partners identically, or whether each
  sees their own stage.
- Whether "elapsed relationship time" is safe. It is a number that only ever
  goes up, which is not a streak, but it is still a number about the
  relationship on a screen.


12. THE SHAREABLE ARTIFACT

12.1 Summary

One thing the product makes that a person would voluntarily post somewhere
else, designed so that sharing it leaks nothing.

12.2 Problem

Per 2.4, a two-person product has a viral coefficient near 1.0 and no network
effect. Growth is paid acquisition or a shareable moment. Monkey Tracker
currently produces no shareable artifact whatsoever.

The category precedent is direct: Agapé grew on TikTok reveal reactions. The
mutual-reveal mechanic in section 8 is the same shape, and it currently
produces nothing anyone can show.

12.3 The tension, stated plainly

An artifact worth sharing is by definition couple content leaving the couple,
and that cuts against every other position in this document. This section is
the least resolved in it, and it should not be built until the tension is
answered rather than assumed away.

The only defensible form is an artifact that is expressive but empty of
substance: it should communicate charm and communicate nothing.

12.4 Design constraints

Any candidate artifact must contain:

- No captions, no answers, no photos, no place names, no timestamps finer
  than a date.
- No display names by default.
- Nothing derived from location at any precision.

And must be:

- Generated only from content that already exists, never from a re-scan of
  expired data.
- Explicitly exported by a person, never auto-posted or auto-suggested.

12.5 Candidates

- A monthly spread: an illustrated poster of the two monkeys across a month,
  built from saved moments. Also the honest way to keep something past
  retention, which ties it to section 11.
- A reveal card from the question jar showing that both answered, with the
  question and without either answer.
- A session card: two monkeys who sat together for fifty minutes.

12.6 Consent

An artifact containing the partner's monkey is partly the partner's. Whether
one person may share it unilaterally is an open question with a defensible
answer in either direction, and it must be decided before the feature exists
rather than discovered afterwards.

12.7 Open questions

- Does the partner approve each share, approve a standing setting, or neither?
- Is a charming artifact that says nothing actually shareable, or does the
  charm live entirely in the substance we are removing?
- Does this feature deserve to exist at all if the honest answer to 12.7's
  second question is no?


13. COMPACT SPECIFICATIONS

Format: what it is, why it earns its place, and the constraint most likely to
kill it.

13.1 Widgets and complications
Extends base 11.9. Home and lock screen widgets showing the partner's monkey,
and a watch complication. Highest value per unit effort here: the illustration
system is already flat, two-tone, and scales to 44px.
Constraint: must render the unknown state as faithfully as the app. A widget
showing a stale monkey because it failed to refresh is the worst available
violation of the expiry rule. Renders a still frame per 3.7, and never shows
live presence per 7.8.

13.2 Push-to-talk walkie
Hold to send three seconds of voice; plays immediately if the partner is
present, otherwise waits as an object on their stage.
Constraint: audio is content. Same private storage handling as photo
postcards, and never autoplays from a notification.

13.3 Banana toss
Throw it across the divider; catchable if the partner is live, landing and
waiting if not.
Constraint: no scores, no records. See 14.2.

13.4 The rope
Both pull, nobody wins, both monkeys wobble.
Constraint: deliberately unwinnable. An outcome becomes a record, and a record
is a scoreboard.

13.5 Heartbeat
From a watch, sent as a haptic rhythm.
Constraint: biometric data. Own consent surface, never stored, transmitted
live only.

13.6 Blind mood match
Each partner picks a mood for the other, revealed together as in section 8.
Constraint: being guessed wrong repeatedly can land badly. No history, no
accuracy score, ever.

13.7 Scratch-off postcards
A photo arrives obscured; rubbing reveals it.
Constraint: the reveal must be skippable. A gesture gate on content someone
wanted becomes friction the second time.

13.8 Doodle replies and doodle telephone
A small drawing appearing as a prop on the partner's stage for an hour, in the
illustration system's ink weight; optionally a guessing game.
Constraint: freeform content needs a moderation story even at a scale of two
people, because two people can still hurt each other.

13.9 Co-drawing on the sky
When both are live, both scribble in one sky; it fades over an hour.
Constraint: as 13.8, plus 6.7's rule against covering controls.

13.10 The evening card
At day's end, one illustrated card of both days side by side.
Constraint: generated from what already exists. If it can be "incomplete", it
is a chore.

13.11 Trail as a walk, not a map
Replace any map surface for Trail with the monkey walking across the stage,
arriving somewhere at the end.
Constraint: the feature most likely to be misread as a safety service. Copy
must be unambiguous that it is a feeling, not a fix.

13.12 Overlap light
A band of light showing when waking hours intersect. Depends on 4.4 Tier 1.
Constraint: must not become a scheduling tool, or it acquires obligations.

13.13 Zero-friction input
NFC stickers, shortcuts, and widget taps that post a preset in one gesture.
Constraint: presets must not drift into automation. An update the user did not
consciously make is a location beacon wearing a costume.

13.14 Postcard as backdrop
An existing private postcard becomes the soft backdrop of that person's half.
Constraint: a backdrop is far more visible than a thumbnail, including to
anyone glancing over a shoulder.

13.15 Ambient sound
Five seconds of ambient audio attached to an update, expiring with it.
Constraint: as 13.2.

13.16 Ask for Perch
A partner may request a precision upgrade for a bounded window; approve or
decline in one tap. Part of the 1.4 differentiator.
Constraint: the app must never surface how often anyone declined, to either
party. Cheap and invisible refusal is the whole feature.

13.17 Fade styles
Choose how an update expires: straight to unknown, or leaving a residue.
Constraint: residue must be visually incapable of reading as current status.

13.18 Letters on a state
Something delivered when the partner is next Free, or next Sleepy.
Constraint: delivery conditions must not create an incentive to set a state.
A letter waiting behind "Free" is a reason to lie about being free.

13.19 The goodnight ritual
Extends base 11.10. Both set Asleep; the stage goes to night together and one
light stays on for whoever wakes first. Overlaps section 5.
Constraint: "who went to bed first" must never become legible as a pattern.

13.20 The monkey frame
A small e-ink desk object showing the partner's current monkey. The
illustration system would render on e-ink with no redesign.
Constraint: a physical always-on display of someone's presence in a shared
room is a different consent question than a phone screen, and needs its own.


14. REJECTED, AND ONE CONFLICT

14.1 Mood-driven weather — rejected
Combining both partners' moods into the stage's weather quietly makes one
person's bad day into the thing that ruined the picture. Guilt with extra
steps. If mood ever drives the sky, it drives only your own half, and never
darkens it.

14.2 Anything that counts — rejected as a category
Unlocks, wardrobes, collections, records, accuracy scores, levels. If the
stage furnishes over time it furnishes from shared moments and never shows a
counter. See 11.5.

14.3 Online status and last-seen — rejected, permanently
Any last-seen timestamp, session history, read receipt, typing indicator, or
"active 4m ago" is rejected outright and not subject to later
reconsideration. The harm is specific and well known — "you were online and
didn't answer me" — and it is precisely the damage this product exists to
avoid.

14.4 Return notifications — rejected as a category
No notification may exist whose purpose is to bring someone back. They
announce that something happened; they never ask for a visit, and they never
reference how long it has been.

14.5 Surprise deliveries — accepted only under two conditions
Rarely and randomly, a firefly or a visiting bird appears on the stage. This is
a variable-ratio reward, which is the slot machine mechanic, and it would
normally be rejected under 14.2. It is defensible only if both hold: it is not
contingent on anything the user did, and it is never notified. That makes it a
reward for a visit already being made rather than a machine for manufacturing
visits. If it acquires a push notification, it is cut.

14.6 Conflict in the base PRD
Base 11.11 "Couple terrarium" has users "earn bananas for posting, reacting,
or completing rituals". Earning currency for posting is a participation
incentive — the mechanism the product's own rules forbid — and the existing
guard that missing a day "does not destroy progress" limits the damage without
removing the incentive. Recommended resolution: accrual from shared moments
both people took part in, never from individual posting frequency, per 11.4
and 11.5.


15. SEQUENCING

Ordered by two rules: mitigate the highest risk first, and defer live
transport as long as possible.

1. Idle life (3). The direct mitigation for 2.1 and 2.2, the largest single
   improvement available, and it needs no new permission, no backend, and no
   live connection.
2. Combination states, first three (5.4). Cheap, renewable, and the reason to
   look at a partner's post rather than read it.
3. Local Sky Tier 1 (4.4). Completes the ambient stage; feeds 3's inputs.
4. Placement (6). The most validated intimacy mechanic in the category, and
   deliberately specified to work without live transport.
5. Widgets (13.1). Highest value per unit effort, and it puts everything above
   where people will see it.
6. Saved moments (11.4, first mechanism only). The cheap half of the month-six
   answer, and a prerequisite for 12.
7. The question jar (8). Strong, independent, and per 8.5 not a substitute for
   any of the above. Needs 8.6's editorial owner assigned first.
8. A stage that furnishes (11.4, second mechanism). Needs 11.7's unpairing
   question answered before it accumulates anything.
9. Live presence Tier 1 (7.4). The first feature requiring live transport.
   Design that channel once, deliberately, here — everything after depends on
   it, and 2.3 and 2.5 both land at this step.
10. Live presence Tiers 2 and 3 (7.4). Haptics and mutual hold.
11. The stage you can both touch (9). Live sync, conflict resolution, an
    accessibility path, and a new composer.
12. Local Sky Tier 2 (4.4). Needs the coarse-locality consent surface designed
    properly; do not rush it behind Tier 1.
13. Sit with me (10). The largest build, and the most dependent on everything
    at step 9.

The shareable artifact (12) is deliberately unscheduled. It should not enter
the queue until 12.7 is answered.

Note the shape: steps 1 through 8 ship with no live transport, no new
permissions beyond timezone, and no persistent connections. That is most of
this document, and it is where the product's problems actually are.


16. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT ANSWER

- Whether any of this changes the Release 0 constraint of exactly one couple.
  Nothing here requires more than two people, and nothing here should be used
  as an argument for relaxing that.
- Growth, beyond section 12 noting that the product has no answer. Paid
  acquisition economics for a two-person app with no network effect are
  unmodelled.
- Cost and load. Live presence, live placement sync, and sessions are a
  persistent connection per user, a different cost shape from expiring rows.
- Native platform work. Widgets, complications, haptics, NFC, watch, and e-ink
  are all outside the Expo-managed surface the app currently lives in.
- Moderation. Several features introduce freeform content between two people.
  Two people can still hurt each other, and nothing in 13.8, 13.9, or 8.5's
  user-written questions should ship without deciding what happens when one
  does.
- What happens at unpairing, which 11.7 raises and does not solve.
