MONKEY TRACKER
Product Requirements Document

Version: 1.0
Status: Draft for MVP
Product type: Mobile app for long-distance couples


1. PRODUCT SUMMARY

Monkey Tracker is a playful presence-sharing app for long-distance couples. It answers “What is my person up to?” without turning the relationship into a surveillance dashboard.

The app combines optional location sharing with expressive check-ins: mood, activity, availability, photos, notes, and a monkey avatar acting out the update. It should feel like Life360 if Life360 were a cute, slightly stupid inside joke between two people.

Example home-screen update:

    Shawn is at the library
    Fighting for his life with electromagnetics
    Mood: crispy
    Free to call after 8:30 PM
    Last updated 12 minutes ago

The product is “performative” because users do not merely transmit coordinates. They stage a tiny version of what they are doing for their partner: choosing a pose, writing a caption, adding a photo, or setting the scene around their monkey.


2. PRODUCT DECISION

Build Couples Mode first.

“Could be for actual monkeys” remains part of the joke, branding, loading messages, and future expansion. It should not become a second set of MVP requirements. A research-oriented Field Notes Mode could be explored later, but couples are the initial customer and use case.


3. PROBLEM

Long-distance couples often want low-effort awareness of each other throughout the day. Existing options are incomplete:

- Location-sharing apps show where someone is, but not what they are doing or how they feel.
- Messaging provides context, but every update requires starting a conversation.
- Social media is too public and polished.
- Repeated “wyd?” messages become repetitive and can feel demanding.
- Constant precise tracking can feel invasive or controlling.

Couples need a private, lightweight way to say, “Here is my little world right now,” and to feel included in their partner’s day without requiring a full conversation.


4. VISION

Make distance feel less empty by turning ordinary life into a shared private performance.

Monkey Tracker should create the feeling of glancing across the room at a partner, not checking a tracking device.


5. GOALS

MVP goals:

1. Let two partners pair into a private shared space in under two minutes.
2. Let either partner publish an expressive presence update in under ten seconds.
3. Make the current state of both partners understandable at a glance.
4. Support optional, consent-based location context without normalizing constant surveillance.
5. Encourage several small, affectionate interactions per day.
6. Give the product a distinctive, screenshot-worthy personality.

Non-goals for MVP:

- Family safety monitoring
- Emergency response or personal-safety guarantees
- Parental tracking
- Public social feeds
- Group relationships or friend groups
- Full messaging replacement
- Fitness tracking
- Actual wildlife GPS tracking
- AI relationship advice
- Desktop or web app


6. TARGET USERS

Primary audience:

- Couples currently in a long-distance relationship
- Couples who travel frequently or have opposite schedules
- College-age and young-adult couples comfortable with widgets, streaks, photos, and playful apps

Secondary audience:

- Couples who live nearby but enjoy ambient updates
- Close friends who want a private shared presence space

Primary job to be done:

    When my partner and I are apart, help me feel naturally included in their ordinary day without forcing either of us to constantly text or surrender all privacy.


7. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

1. Expression over surveillance
   A status should communicate more than a dot on a map.

2. Consent must be obvious
   Users always know what is being shared, with whom, at what precision, and for how long.

3. Tiny interactions count
   A reaction, pose, or one-line update should feel meaningful.

4. Cute, not childish
   The monkeys are expressive and funny, but the product should still feel designed for adults.

5. No punishment for having a life
   Avoid guilt-heavy alerts, partner comparisons, or aggressive streak loss.

6. Private by default
   One couple, no follower counts, no public discovery, and no engagement farming.


8. BRAND AND PRODUCT LANGUAGE

Working name: Monkey Tracker

Possible tagline:

    See what your monkey is up to.

Suggested product vocabulary:

- Monkey: a user/partner
- Troop: the private space shared by a couple
- Perch: a saved place such as Home, Work, Campus, or Gym
- Monkey Update: a presence post
- Trail: temporary live-location sharing
- Banana: lightweight reward/currency
- Poke: a request for an update
- Monkey Business: the shared update timeline

Tone:

- Affectionate
- Absurd
- Self-aware
- Occasionally deadpan
- Never clinical, accusatory, or safety-alarm oriented

Example copy:

- “Your monkey has entered the library.”
- “No recent monkey business.”
- “Devin is probably still at work. Last update: 42 min ago.”
- “Trail disappeared into the jungle.”
- “Poke sent. Do not abuse your power.”


9. CORE EXPERIENCE

The home screen is a small stage containing both users’ monkey avatars.

Each monkey appears in a scene based on its latest update. For example, a monkey studying at the library might sit under a desk lamp surrounded by papers. A monkey at the gym might lift a comically small dumbbell. A monkey marked “sleeping” curls up in a blanket.

The partner card shows:

- Current place or intentionally hidden location
- Current activity
- Mood
- Availability to text/call
- Optional caption or photo
- Time of last update
- Whether the update was manual or inferred

The map is secondary. It provides location context when enabled, but the expressive stage is the default view.


10. LOCATION MODEL

Monkey Tracker must support multiple levels of sharing instead of treating precise, continuous location as the default.

Level 0 — Hidden
No location is shared. The user can still publish mood, activity, and availability.

Level 1 — Perch
The user manually selects a named place such as “Campus,” “Work,” or “The Zoo.” No coordinates are shown.

Level 2 — Nearby
The app shares an approximate area or neighborhood. Exact coordinates are not shown.

Level 3 — Trail
Precise live location is shared for a user-selected duration, such as 15 minutes, 1 hour, or until arrival.

Requirements:

- The selected location level is always visible to the sharing user.
- Trail automatically expires.
- Every location display includes a “last updated” timestamp.
- A partner cannot enable or increase another user’s sharing level.
- Turning off sharing takes effect immediately.
- No invisible tracking, stealth mode, or notification when a partner disables sharing.
- The app must not imply that stale location data is current.


11. MVP FEATURES

P0 — Required for launch

11.1 Account and pairing

- Sign up using phone number, email, Apple, or Google.
- Create a display name and choose/customize a monkey avatar.
- Invite exactly one partner through a private link or six-digit code.
- Both users explicitly accept the pairing.
- Either user can leave the troop at any time.
- Pairing data becomes inaccessible to the former partner after separation.

11.2 Monkey Updates

A user can publish an update containing:

- Activity from a short preset list or custom text
- Mood from emoji-style options or custom text
- Availability: free, text only, busy, or asleep
- Optional caption
- Optional photo
- Location level and place
- Expiration time: 30 minutes, 2 hours, end of day, or custom
- Monkey pose/scene selected automatically with manual override

Publishing should require no more than three taps when using recent choices.

11.3 Shared home stage

- Show both avatars and their latest active updates.
- Visually translate activities and moods into poses/props.
- Display timestamps and sharing precision clearly.
- Tap either avatar to see the full update.
- If an update expires, show the state as unknown instead of retaining it as current.

11.4 Reactions

- React to an update with a small set of affectionate or funny reactions.
- Allow one short reply of up to 140 characters.
- Animate reactions on the recipient’s monkey/scene.
- Do not build threaded chat in MVP.

11.5 Pokes

- Send a playful request for a fresh update.
- Limit pokes to prevent spam, initially three per user per day.
- Recipient can mute pokes or all nonessential notifications.
- A poke never demands location specifically.

11.6 Monkey Business timeline

- Private chronological history of updates, photos, reactions, and shared moments.
- Default retention: 30 days.
- Users can save individual moments to a shared scrapbook.
- Either user can delete their own update.

11.7 Notifications

- Partner posted an update
- Partner reacted or replied
- Partner sent a poke
- Optional reminder to post an update
- Trail is about to expire
- Trail has ended

All nonessential notifications are individually configurable.

11.8 Privacy and relationship controls

- Per-update location precision
- Global location-sharing off switch
- Block/unpair flow
- Report flow
- Data export and account deletion
- Photo-download permission setting
- Clear explanation of what the partner can currently see


P1 — Strong follow-up features

11.9 Home-screen widget

- Shows partner’s avatar, activity, mood, and last update.
- Hides sensitive location details on the lock screen by default.

11.10 Shared rituals

- Good morning / good night buttons
- “Home safe” signal
- Daily question
- One-photo-a-day ritual
- Countdown to next visit

11.11 Couple terrarium

- Shared decorative habitat grows through mutual participation.
- Users earn bananas for posting, reacting, or completing rituals.
- Bananas unlock cosmetic items only.
- Missing a day does not destroy progress or publicly blame either user.

11.12 Smart scenes

- Scene automatically changes based on activity, time, mood, and place.
- Users can correct the scene when the guess is wrong.


P2 — Future exploration

- Multiple visual themes and non-monkey avatars
- Visit planning and travel countdowns
- Shared playlists
- Weekly “Monkey Recap” generated from saved moments
- Optional friend mode
- Actual Monkey Mode: field notes, sightings, photos, tags, and research exports


12. PRIMARY USER FLOWS

12.1 First-time setup

1. User downloads the app.
2. User creates an account.
3. User chooses a monkey and display name.
4. User creates a troop and sends an invite.
5. Partner accepts.
6. Both users independently choose their default sharing level.
7. The app prompts each person to publish a first update.
8. Both monkeys appear together on the home stage.

Success condition: both partners are paired and have posted within five minutes.

12.2 Post an update

1. User taps their monkey or the “Update” button.
2. User selects activity, mood, and availability.
3. User optionally adds a caption, photo, place, or Trail.
4. User previews the resulting scene.
5. User publishes.
6. Partner receives a notification based on their preferences.

Success condition: a returning user can publish a basic update in under ten seconds.

12.3 Check on a partner

1. User opens the app or widget.
2. Partner’s stage shows the latest update and its age.
3. User taps for details or reacts directly.
4. If the update is stale, the app offers a gentle poke.

Success condition: user understands the partner’s current shared context without opening a map.

12.4 Share a Trail

1. User selects Trail from the update composer.
2. User chooses a duration or destination-based expiration.
3. App displays exactly what will be shared.
4. User confirms.
5. Partner can view the live trail until expiration.
6. Sharing user can end it at any time.

Success condition: precise sharing always has informed consent and a clear ending.


13. USER STORIES

- As a long-distance partner, I want to see what my person is doing so I feel included in their day.
- As a busy user, I want to post an update quickly so sharing does not become another chore.
- As a private person, I want to share my mood without sharing my exact location.
- As a traveling user, I want to temporarily share my route and arrival so my partner can follow along.
- As a partner, I want to react to tiny moments without starting a full conversation.
- As a user, I want old updates to expire so they are not mistaken for my current state.
- As a user leaving a relationship, I want to revoke access immediately and cleanly.
- As a couple, we want to collect ordinary moments into something sentimental over time.


14. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

- Mobile support: iOS and Android.
- Two-person private spaces only for MVP.
- New updates should appear for the partner within five seconds under normal network conditions.
- Update timestamps must reflect the server time of the last successful update.
- Offline posts should be labeled pending and sent when connectivity returns.
- Location permissions must use native OS permission flows.
- Background location is requested only when a user explicitly starts a Trail requiring it.
- Expired updates must not appear as current.
- Photos must support removal and basic content reporting.
- A user’s block or unpair action must immediately stop future data access.
- Push notifications must avoid exposing sensitive captions or places unless the user opts in.


15. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Performance:

- Home screen interactive within two seconds on a typical connection.
- Update composer opens within 500 milliseconds after initial load.
- Graceful degraded experience when location or notification permission is denied.

Reliability:

- Target 99.9% service uptime after beta.
- Failed updates remain editable and retryable.
- Trail expiration is enforced server-side, not only by the client.

Security and privacy:

- Encrypt data in transit and at rest.
- Store the minimum location history required for the feature.
- Do not sell location or relationship data.
- Do not use location, moods, photos, or captions for ad targeting.
- Maintain audit logs for pairing, unpairing, and sharing-setting changes.
- Provide simple account deletion and reasonable data-retention limits.

Accessibility:

- Do not rely on color alone for mood, availability, or location state.
- Support screen readers, dynamic text, reduced motion, and high contrast.
- All monkey animations have text equivalents.


16. SAFETY AND ABUSE PREVENTION

Location products can enable coercive control. The following are product requirements, not optional polish:

- Sharing is off until each user chooses otherwise.
- No “Why did they stop sharing?” alert.
- No score for hours spent sharing precise location.
- No alerts for unexpected travel or deviation from routine.
- No ability to remotely change a partner’s settings.
- No repeated system pressure to enable precise location.
- Fast, discreet blocking and unpairing.
- Safety resources accessible from privacy settings.
- Sensitive content is hidden from notification previews by default.
- Internal support tools must restrict and log access to location data.

The app must clearly state that it is not an emergency service and that location may be delayed, unavailable, or inaccurate.


17. BUSINESS MODEL

Recommended model: freemium subscription for the troop.

Free:

- Pairing
- Presence updates
- Basic scenes and avatars
- Reactions and pokes
- Perch and limited Trails
- 30-day timeline

Monkey Plus, approximately $3.99–$5.99 per month per couple:

- Premium monkey outfits and habitats
- Longer scrapbook history
- Additional widgets
- Visit countdowns and weekly recaps
- More custom places and scene packs
- Higher-quality photo storage

Do not monetize through advertising or sale of personal/location data.


18. SUCCESS METRICS

North-star metric:

    Weekly reciprocating troops: paired couples where both partners post or react on at least three separate days in a week.

Activation:

- Invite acceptance rate
- Percentage of new troops where both partners post within 24 hours
- Median time from first signup to paired first update

Engagement:

- Updates per active troop per day
- Percentage of updates receiving a reaction
- Active days per troop per week
- Widget adoption
- Ritual completion rate

Retention:

- Week 1, Week 4, and Week 12 troop retention
- Percentage of active troops with participation from both partners

Trust and safety guardrails:

- Unpair/block rate after sharing prompts
- Percentage of users who understand their current sharing level in usability testing
- Location-related support and abuse reports
- Accidental background-location incidents
- Percentage of Trails that end at the selected time

21. TECHNICAL ASSUMPTIONS

Suggested implementation for a small team:

- Client: React Native with Expo
- Backend: Supabase or Firebase
- Authentication: native email/social authentication
- Realtime updates: Supabase Realtime or Firestore listeners
- Maps: Mapbox, Google Maps, or Apple Maps depending on cost and platform needs
- Push: Expo Notifications initially, native services as scale requires
- Media: private object storage with signed access
- Analytics: privacy-conscious event analytics with precise location excluded

This is not a final architecture. Background location behavior, battery impact, map pricing, privacy compliance, and store-review rules require technical validation before committing to a provider.


22. LAUNCH ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The MVP is ready to launch when:

- Two new users can pair without staff assistance.
- Both users can publish and receive updates reliably.
- Users can understand the difference between Hidden, Perch, Nearby, and Trail.
- Trail ends automatically and immediately when manually stopped.
- Expired updates cannot be mistaken for current information.
- Blocking/unpairing revokes access in tested scenarios.
- The main flow works when location permission is denied.
- Notification previews are private by default.
- Core screens pass accessibility checks.
- Crash-free session rate meets the beta target.
- At least 70% of beta couples say the app helps them feel more present in each other’s day.


23. OPEN QUESTIONS

1. Is the home experience primarily a stage, a map, or a vertically split combination?
2. Should updates expire by default, or remain until replaced?
3. How much monkey customization is needed for emotional attachment at launch?
4. Should a troop subscription be purchasable by either partner and cover both?
5. Are exact live Trails necessary for MVP, or should the first release stop at user-selected Perches?
6. Should the app support best friends at launch without changing the couples-focused marketing?
7. How silly can notification copy be before it becomes annoying?
8. What data, if any, should survive unpairing for a user’s personal archive?


24. ONE-SENTENCE PITCH

Monkey Tracker is a private app where long-distance couples turn their location, mood, and everyday activities into cute live scenes—so checking in feels affectionate instead of invasive.
